## Why Executive Team Building is a Strategic Imperative

At the top of any organization, where the decisions that shape an entire company's direction are made, team cohesion is more critical than anywhere else. Yet, paradoxically, it is often within Executive Committees — the CODIR in French corporate culture — that relational dysfunctions carry the heaviest consequences and prove the most challenging to address.

Members of an executive committee are, by definition, accomplished leaders in their respective fields. Each one leads their own team, carries their own vision, and champions their own priorities. This individual strength becomes a collective obstacle when it is not channeled into an authentic spirit of cooperation. Leadership meetings transform into negotiation arenas, strategic decisions stall, and a shared vision fades behind compartmentalized interests.

Executive team building is not entertainment — it is an organizational performance tool. In Tunisia, where businesses navigate rapidly evolving economic environments, cohesion at the top is not a luxury: it is a competitive necessity that separates high-performing organizations from those that struggle to execute their strategies.

## The Specific Challenges of Executive Teams

### The Leader-in-a-Team Paradox

Chief executives, financial directors, commercial leaders and other executive committee members have built their careers on individual performance, decision-making autonomy, and internal competition. The very qualities that elevated them to senior positions can become barriers to genuine collaboration when the challenge is to function as a real, unified team.

Competition for budget resources, disagreements on strategic priorities, or differing visions for the company's direction create latent tensions within the leadership group. These tensions, left unmanaged, accumulate over time and degrade the quality of collective decisions, slow down the execution of strategic plans, and send a signal of dysfunction that the entire organization perceives — even from a distance.

Research in organizational psychology consistently shows that teams composed entirely of high performers can underperform relative to their individual talent level when the relational dynamics between members are dysfunctional. The executive committee is the prime example of this phenomenon.

### The Downstream Impact of Leadership Cohesion

Management research is unequivocal: the quality of dynamics within an executive team has a direct, measurable impact on the performance of the entire organization. When executive committee members display unity of vision and consistency in their decisions, operational teams work more efficiently, cross-functional projects advance more rapidly, and the company culture gains in clarity and coherence.

Conversely, when friction at the top becomes perceptible — contradictory messages, constantly challenged priorities, interminable arbitration processes — it is the entire organization that bears the consequences: declining employee motivation, eroding trust in leadership, and a progressive deterioration of collective performance. The financial cost of a dysfunctional executive committee, measured in delayed decisions, failed projects, and talent attrition, far exceeds the investment required to address the root causes.

## Executive Team Building: A Differentiated Approach

### What Sets Executive Programs Apart

Team building designed for executive teams differs fundamentally from the generic programs offered to operational teams. It must account for the particular psychological profile of senior leaders: their need for autonomy, their relationship with risk, their sensitivity to issues of status and power, and their naturally long-term temporal horizon.

An effective executive team building program does not simply organize enjoyable activities. It creates situations where leaders are invited to observe their own collective functioning, identify the dynamics that limit their effectiveness as a team, and develop concrete practices they can transpose into their daily interactions.

The best programs combine experiential learning — activities that create genuine emotional engagement — with structured reflection facilitated by experts in organizational dynamics. This combination ensures that the insights generated during the program translate into lasting behavioral changes rather than fading within days of returning to the office.

### Formats That Work for Executive Committees

Several formats have proven their value for executive teams in Tunisia. Residential seminars of two to three days, in an inspiring natural setting far from the usual corporate environment, allow participants to step back from operational urgency and engage in meaningful work on their collective dynamic. Long-term cohesion programs, with regular sequences spread over six to twelve months, anchor new practices over time and allow the organization to observe their actual transformation in day-to-day interactions.

## Recommended Activities for Executive Committees

### Strategic Simulations and Decision-Making Exercises

Management simulations — where executive committee members jointly manage a fictional crisis, pilot a simulated company, or negotiate complex decisions in a constrained environment — are particularly effective for leadership teams. These exercises reveal the natural operating modes of each member, the influence dynamics at play within the group, and the collective blind spots that need to be addressed.

When followed by a structured debriefing session facilitated by an experienced practitioner, these simulations generate deep, shared insights. Participants leave with a renewed understanding of their colleagues and concrete levers to improve their collaboration. This type of exercise also allows teams to simulate high-pressure situations — a reputational crisis, a sudden market reversal, strategic decision-making under deep uncertainty — that test the executive committee's collective resilience.

### Outdoor Challenges and Physical Activities

Physical activities — hiking, sailing, horseback riding, outdoor challenges — play an important role in building trust among executives. They create situations of relative equality where habitual hierarchies temporarily recede, enabling more authentic interactions. Collectively overcoming a physical challenge or an outdoor trial generates shared emotions that strengthen interpersonal bonds in durable ways.

In Tunisia, the country's exceptional landscape diversity provides outstanding settings for these activities. The verdant ridges of the northern Tell mountains, the Mediterranean coastline with its varied beaches, or the Saharan oases of the south each offer a unique experience. These natural environments leave lasting impressions and create strong collective memories that become shared reference points during future working meetings — a kind of team mythology that reinforces identity and belonging.

### Vision Workshops and Collective Intelligence Sessions

Strategic vision-building workshops are particularly well-suited to executive committees. These creative working sessions allow leadership team members to align their representations of the company's future, identify areas of tension in their respective priorities, and co-construct a shared narrative that will give coherence to their future decisions.

These workshops, facilitated by collective intelligence experts, combine methods drawn from design thinking, lean management, and systemic coaching to produce concrete, actionable results. They often help to defuse latent strategic disagreements that, for lack of a safe space in which to be expressed, were weighing on the atmosphere of leadership meetings and slowing collective decision-making.

## Selecting the Right Partner for Executive Team Building

### Key Selection Criteria

Organizing a team building program for an executive team requires specific expertise and a genuinely customized approach. Generalist providers offering standardized activity catalogs are typically not equipped to address the particular needs of a senior leadership group. Organizations should seek partners who combine expertise in organizational development, executive coaching, and high-level group facilitation.

Discretion is also an essential criterion: the group dynamics within an executive committee touch on sensitive issues — complex interpersonal relationships, peer conflict management, power and succession questions — that require absolute confidentiality from service providers. An experienced professional will navigate these subjects with the necessary delicacy and skill.

### The Role of the Expert Facilitator

The quality of the facilitator is often the single most determining factor in the success of an executive team building program. This professional must combine solid experience in executive coaching, a mastery of group dynamics, and the ability to navigate high-relational-pressure environments with ease and credibility.

They must be capable of creating sufficient psychological safety for executive committee members to speak candidly about the challenges of their collective functioning, while maintaining the process within a productive, results-oriented framework. A facilitator who lacks credibility with a room full of senior leaders — or who fails to maintain the balance between challenge and safety — will find the program undermined from within.

## Measuring the Impact of an Executive Program

### Quantitative Indicators

The impact of an executive team building program can be measured through several quantitative indicators: the average duration of decision-making processes within the CODIR, the frequency and duration of inter-directorate conflicts, the advancement rate of cross-functional projects, and the satisfaction of managerial teams regarding the clarity of strategic direction provided by the executive committee.

These data points, collected before and after the program, make it possible to objectify the value added by the investment and to justify the approach to shareholders or the board of directors. Many organizations also track leading indicators during the program itself — engagement scores, quality of dialogue assessments — that provide early signals of the program's effectiveness.

### Qualitative Indicators

Qualitative indicators are equally revealing: the quality of dialogue during executive committee meetings, the authenticity of exchanges between members, the collective capacity to address difficult subjects without avoidance, and the coherence perceived by organizational teams in the strategic messages carried by each member of the executive committee.

A qualitative audit conducted six months after the program, through individual interviews with executive committee members and their direct teams, allows the organization to assess the durability of the changes introduced and to adjust the program accordingly.

## Integrating Team Building into a Sustainable Leadership Strategy

### A Continuous Process, Not a One-Off Event

The most common mistake organizations make is treating executive team building as a one-off event — an annual seminar — rather than as a continuous process of collective leadership development. The highest-performing executive teams maintain their cohesion through regular rituals: executive committee meetings that include dedicated time for reflection on collective functioning, ongoing coaching sequences for the group as a whole, and recurring thematic seminars.

### Aligning Team Building with Strategic Priorities

The executive team building program must be aligned with the organization's strategic priorities. If the company is going through a phase of digital transformation, the chosen activities and workshops should foster collective agility and change management capability. If it is embarking on international expansion, the program should reinforce strategic alignment and the capacity to communicate a coherent vision to diverse stakeholders across different cultural contexts.

A preliminary diagnosis of the current dynamics within the executive committee — conducted through individual interviews and observation of leadership meetings — is often the recommended first step in designing a program that is genuinely adapted to the team's specific needs. This diagnostic phase prevents the all-too-common pitfall of applying a generic solution to a unique organizational situation.
